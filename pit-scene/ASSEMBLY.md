# Theme 1 corridor kit: assembly guide for the Unity implementer

The pit is an endless treadmill corridor (spec.md §1.3a, style.md §1):
- There is no ceiling and no far wall. The far end dissolves into fog.
- The floor and both side walls scroll toward the player at 1 cell per turn and recycle in groups of 4 rows.
- The wall look moves from the town-side upper foundry (start), through the foundry proper (mid), to the approach to the boss lair (end).
- A casting-pit gate scrolls in out of the fog before the final boss and stops just beyond the board.

Everything here is generated by scripts in `tools/`, so it can be rebuilt (from `pit-scene/`):
```
/usr/bin/python3 tools/make_atlas.py                # textures/T1_Scenery_Atlas.png (needs PIL; Homebrew python3 lacks it)
/opt/homebrew/bin/blender -b -P tools/build_kit.py  # kit/fbx, kit/glb, kit/t1_kit.blend, kit/kit_report.json
/opt/homebrew/bin/blender -b kit/t1_kit.blend -P tools/assemble.py -- --cols 7 --rows 9 --turn 1 --balls --out preview/t1_corridor_7x9_start.png
```
`tools/assemble.py` is the reference implementation of the rules below (`layout_corridor()`, `pick_segments()`, `group_progress()`). If anything here is unclear, read it.

Lighting, fog and camera values are in [LIGHTING.md](LIGHTING.md). The camera is style.md §2: perspective, 30° down from behind and above the player.

\*Unity verts are counted after hard-normal and UV splits: unique (vertex, face normal, uv). Every piece is ≤ 2000 (style.md §8). The pieces are flat-shaded on purpose, so import with Normals: Import.

- **Seamless joins:**
  - Each segment's first and last cell is plain brick with the same profile: plinth, face, soot band and coping at the same heights and depths.
  - The brick texture restarts on every cell.
  - So **any segment joins any other** along the corridor, including across sets.
  - The bulky features (pier, buttress, breach) always sit in cells 2–3.
- **End-set feel:** the end set is deeper and taller (1.72 deep, 5.75 tall), with damaged brick, buttresses and heavy chains. The corridor visibly closes in near the boss.
- Per-piece files contain no textures. `kit/T1_Kit_All_preview.glb` holds every piece with the atlas embedded, for viewing only. Don't ship it.

## 2. Frame and grid
- **1 cell = 1.125 units** (spec §0). **1 group = 4 rows = 4.5 units = one wall segment length = one floor strip.**
- Make an empty `PitRoot` with position = board centre at the play plane, rotation = **(−90, 0, 0)**.
  - This is for the game's frame: the board in world XY, the camera looking along +Z.
  - Its local +Y then points at the camera, and its local +Z points screen-up, toward the far end.
  - All numbers below are PitRoot-local.
- Row-cell centres sit at z = k × 1.125 for integer k. Odd row counts are centred on k = 0. The board's far row is k = (rows−1)/2.
- The wall collision line (the ball-bounce edge) is at x = ±cols/2 × 1.125:

| Board | Wall face x |
|---|---|
| 7×9 | ±3.9375 |
| 9×11 | ±5.0625 |
| 11×13 | ±6.1875 |
| 13×15 | ±7.3125 |

- **Live groups:** 9 groups are alive at once. Screen slots g = −4…+4 cover cells k = 4g…4g+3, which is k −16…19: from below the screen bottom to past the fog end at 13×15.

## 3. Pivots and orientation
- **Segments:**
  - The origin is at floor level, at the centre of the segment along the wall (between cells 2 and 3), on the collision face.
  - **Prefab forward (+Z) points into the board.** The wall body extends behind (−Z).
  - Local x spans [−2.25, +2.25]. Cell 1 is at local −X in Blender, which is Unity +X, so the cell order flips on the right wall. Nothing depends on that order.
  - Below a height of 1.5, nothing sticks out in front of the collision face, so balls and units at the board edge never overlap the wall. Only the coping and loose bricks project, higher up.
- **Floor strips:** the origin is at the strip centre (the group centre), face up. The strip spans cols × 4 cells.
- **Gate tower:** the origin is at floor level, at the centre of its front face. It is symmetric, so both sides use rotation 0.
- **Gate lintel:** the origin is at floor level, at the centre of its front face. It spans 17 units across.
- **Import check:** `T1_Start_B` should be 4.5 wide, with its brick face toward +Z. If the face points −Z, rotate the model child 180° on Y and report it. The GLB was verified numerically (glTF +Z = into the board).

## 4. Building and scrolling the corridor
### 4.1 Placement of one group
For a group whose centre row is `kc = 4g + 1.5` (screen slot g, see 4.2):
```
zc = kc * 1.125
left  segment: position (-cols/2*1.125, 0, zc), rotY = +90     (prefab forward -> +X, into the board)
right segment: position (+cols/2*1.125, 0, zc), rotY = -90
floor strip  : position (0, 0, zc), T1_FloorStrip_W{cols}_B if hash(seed, G, 7) % 3 == 0 else _A
```
Floor strip by board width (swap all strips on expansion, §4.4):

| Board | Strip |
|---|---|
| 7×9 | `T1_FloorStrip_W7_A/B` |
| 9×11 | `T1_FloorStrip_W9_A/B` |
| 11×13 | `T1_FloorStrip_W11_A/B` |
| 13×15 | `T1_FloorStrip_W13_A/B` |

Make the left segment, right segment and floor strip children of one `Group` object, so scrolling moves one transform per group. In `assemble.py` the y coordinate is the pit's along-axis; in Unity it is PitRoot-local z.

### 4.2 Scroll (spec §1.3a)
- Keep one float `scroll` in cells. It rises by **1 per turn**, animated over the turn's advance and driven by the combat clock.
  - It freezes while paused.
  - It stops when the final boss arrives (the gate has docked, see 4.5).
  - On win or lose it decelerates to 0 at 1 cell/s².
  - In endless mode it resumes at 1 cell per turn.
  - Mid-run bosses don't stop it.
- A group with absolute index `G` (an integer that never changes while the group lives) sits at screen slot `g = G − scroll/4`. Its centre is `z = (4G + 1.5 − scroll) × 1.125`.
- **Recycling:** when a group has fully passed below the near end (`4G + 3 − scroll < −16`), set `G += 9`, which moves it 40.5 units to the far end, and **redraw it**: pick new segments and a new floor strip for the new G. The far end is deep in fog (fully fogged from k ≈ 12 at 7×9), so the swap is never seen.
- Use absolute `G` in every hash, not the screen slot, so a group keeps its look while it scrolls. (`assemble.py` renders one frozen moment, so there G = g.)

### 4.3 Which set a group uses (progress)
Progress is **time-based, not spatial** (spec §1.3a: the turn number the row corresponds to, divided by the final boss turn). A group's progress is the turn at which its rows reach the board's far row, over the final boss turn:
```
row_turn = turn_now + (kc_screen - rows/2)       // kc_screen = 4g + 1.5 when the group is (re)drawn
p        = clamp(row_turn / final_turn, 0, 1)    // final_turn = turn the final boss arrives (preview: 60)
p       += ((h % 1000) / 1000 - 0.5) * 2 * 0.05  // h = hash(seed, G, side); a ±0.05 jitter so the set change is ragged
set      = p < 0.3 ? start : p < 0.7 ? mid : end
```
- Compute p once, when the group is drawn.
  - `row_turn` stays the same for a group while it scrolls: turn and screen position move together.
  - So drawing it at the far end is the same as computing it on screen.
- Pick a segment within the set:
  - `i = (h >> 10) % n`.
  - Step `i = (i + 1) % n` while the pick equals the left segment of this group (for the right side), or equals the same side's segment in the previous group (G − 1), when n ≥ 3.
  - So left ≠ right, and no segment appears twice in a row on one side.
- Hash: FNV-1a style, unchecked `uint`:
  ```
  h = 2166136261
  for each x: h = (h ^ (uint)x) * 16777619
  ```
  With x = seed, G, 'L' or 'R' (76/82), and 7 for the floor.
- Endless mode: p is clamped at 1, so the end set continues.

### 4.4 Board expansion (7×9 → 9×11 → 11×13 → 13×15, spec §1.3)
Each expansion adds a column on each side. The 3-second animation runs with the camera pull-back:
1. **Walls:** tween every left segment's x by −1.125 and every right segment's by +1.125. One tween per side if the segments are parented under a `LeftWall` / `RightWall` (then put the floor strip under the Group only).
2. **Floor:** at the start of the tween, swap every group's floor strip to the next width, `W{cols_new}`. The extra outer column starts hidden under the wall body, which is ≥ 0.97 deep, and the sliding walls uncover it.
3. **Rows:** nothing to do. The live run (k −16…19) already covers the 15-row board plus the near margin and the fogged far end.
4. **Camera, fog and spot:** recompute and tween them as LIGHTING.md says.
5. Newly recycled groups use the new cols at once.

Collision walls stay in game code (2D), at the same x values as the wall faces.

### 4.5 End-of-corridor gate (the boss emerges from it)
- Spec §1.3a: when the final boss enters, the scroll speed drops to 0 and the boss walks from the corridor end onto the board. The gate is that end. It rides in out of the fog on the last few turns before the final boss, and it is docked exactly when the scroll stops.
- The gate's front face is at `k_gate = rows/2 + 3 + (final_turn − turn)`. It scrolls with the corridor and **stops 3 cells beyond the board's far row** on the final boss turn.
- Spawn it once `k_gate ≤ 20`, the far end of the live run. It enters out of the fog.
- Positions:
  - towers at x = ±(cols/2 × 1.125 + 1.2);
  - lintel at x = 0, with `scale.x = (cols × 1.125 + 2.4) / 17`;
  - all at z = k_gate × 1.125, rotation 0, as one `Gate` parent.
- Rescale the lintel when the board expands.
- **Set Cast Shadows Off on all gate renderers.** Otherwise the lintel throws a shadow band across the board.
- The towers sit behind the wall line. At the fitted camera, mostly the lintel and its chains show: an arch over the corridor, fading into fog, with the boss stepping out under it. See `preview/t1_corridor_13x15_final.png`.
- Endless mode: let the gate scroll on with the corridor. Destroy it once it passes k < −20.

### 4.6 Cost
- Worst case, at 13×15 with all heavy end segments: 18 segments × about 1500 + 9 × 208 floor + 1424 gate ≈ **30k verts**.
- Typical: about 15–20k.
- One material, so batching is easy. Static batching does not survive moving groups. Use the SRP Batcher (default in URP) or GPU instancing on the shared material. The pieces are linked duplicates of 9 + 8 + 2 meshes.
- Optional: disable groups with k > rows/2 + 12; they are fully fogged.

## 5. Material and atlas
- **One material for every piece: `T1_Scenery`.**
  - Shader: URP/Lit (or Simple Lit). Base Map = `textures/T1_Scenery_Atlas.png`, colour white.
  - Metallic 0, Smoothness 0.1, no normal map, no emission. GPU Instancing on.
- Import settings for each FBX/GLB:
  - Scale factor 1 (FBX: Convert Units on).
  - Materials: **None**, or remap to `T1_Scenery`.
  - Normals: Import. Read/Write off. Mesh compression Medium.
- Atlas import: Default, sRGB on, no alpha, max 1024, Clamp, Bilinear, mipmaps on, ASTC 6×6 (ETC2 fallback). About 64 KB as PNG, about 0.2 MB compressed.
- Atlas layout (pixels, top-left origin; defined in `tools/make_atlas.py` and `tools/build_kit.py`):

| Region | Pixels | Content |
|---|---|---|
| brick A / B | 0–255 / 256–511 × 0–1023 | one cell of wall face (1.125 × 4.5); B is damaged |
| stone | 512–767 × 0–511 | dressed blocks (piers, buttresses, gate) |
| floor A / B | 512–767 × 512–767 / 768–1023 | one cell each; tiles in every direction |
| iron / clay / bronze | 768–1023, 256 px bands | material tiles |
| swatches | 768–1023 × 768–831 | 16 flat 32 px colours |

- Values: V p5/p50/p95 = 8 / 18 / 29 %, S p50/p95 = 30 / 44 %, inside style.md §4's scenery range.
